Terrain-Aware Counter-UAS System

A high-altitude anti-drone system that senses at wavelengths terrain and weather don’t block: an acoustic array designed to hear rotors past a ridge, and a satellite forward-scatter gate at mountain passes. It detects and cues, adding to DRDO’s existing D4-class chain rather than replacing it.

01 · The problem at 4,500–5,000 m

Problem

01Degradation

Cold, thin air, icing and dust wear down the hardware. Hardening fixes this.

02Occlusion

Ridges block line of sight; fog and snow blind the optics. Hardening cannot fix this, and in mountains it dominates.

Key insight: sense at wavelengths that terrain and weather don’t block.

02 · Terrain-aware C-UAS

System

The terrain picks the sensing wave, and the elevation map (CartoDEM) is an input to fusion. The chain adds to DRDO’s existing D4-class system; it does not replace it.

Corridor gate

  • Drone crossing at a pass
  • Doppler signature

Acoustic array

  • Bearing past the ridge
  • 3-node triangulation for range

DEM terrain mask

  • Flags whether the drone is in line of sight

Cue

Line of sight
D4 EO/IR and laser
No line of sight
Gate jammer (Idea B) or crest head (Idea C)

Detect and cue. Neutralisation means re-siting and cueing existing soft-kill, not building a new weapon. The gate jammer denies the route; it does not down the drone.

Five contributions

01Diffraction as the design point

Sound bending over the ridge is what the system relies on, not an impairment it fights.

02Two altitude errors, found and fixed

A sound-speed bearing bias of up to ~12°, and a ×1.29 rotor-tone shift.

03Nested array Built

A compact core rules out ghost targets; far pods on unequal arms give precision; all on one clock. Self-survey and self-measured wind are on the roadmap.

04DEM-aware localisation Roadmap

Treats the terrain as a known lens, predicting how the bearing bends over a crest.

05Silent corridor gate Designed

A receive-only gate that keeps working alongside our own jammer.

03 · Idea 1 (primary) · Ridge-diffraction acoustic array

Array

Built prototype · the “nested array”

8Microphones, two boards, one clock

Built · sea-level tested

Hub core0.30 m regular tetrahedron of 4 microphones on a 0.8 m mast
Outrigger pods4 pods on unequal arms of 1.1 / 1.7 / 2.3 / 3.1 m
Microphones8 × INMP441 I²S MEMS, digitised at the capsule (no analog cable)
Boards2 × ESP32-S3 (4 capsules each) on a shared clock
MaterialsAl 6061-T6 frame and enclosure · graphene clear coat · closed-cell PU insulation foam

No performance figures are claimed for the graphene coat.

Shared clock between boards

  • Board 1 is the clock master; board 2 runs as a slave
  • A READY handshake: no clock edge exists until both boards are listening
  • Every packet carries an absolute frame index; the host merges the streams on it

Long runs to the pods

  • Plain I²S can’t run 3 m, so the pods use differential RS-422 over Cat5e
  • Blue pair BCLK · orange WS · green SD/READY · brown power/ground
  • AM26LV31/32 or MAX3490 line drivers (MAX485 is too slow); 74LVC157 multiplexer at the hub

Processing

  • GCC-PHAT time-delay estimation, band-limited on the cross-spectrum over 80 Hz–4 kHz. A known bug is fixed: filtering before PHAT inverts the delay
  • Harmonic-comb detector finds any rotor or propeller harmonic series, and rejects generator hum because hum is too stable
  • Temperature-corrected sound speed
  • Echoes off rock faces show up as separate lag peaks; only lags consistent across all microphone pairs are kept

Built-in

  • Self-test (BIT) every 10 minutes
  • Jammer BLANK input mutes processing while our own jammer fires

Range: triangulation across 3 nodes, 150–200 m apart

≈ 3%range error at 200 m
≈ 9%range error at 500 m
≈ 17%range error at 1 km

Targets, assuming 2° bearing accuracy. Spectral-tilt ranging was dropped because it saturates.

Roadmap · not what is built

The field node replaces core-plus-pods with two 0.25 m-edge tetrahedra, each with its own ESP32-S3 and LiFePO4 battery, joined by a shared RS-422 clock and an acoustically measured 4 m baseline. Sampling at 48 kHz.

At −30 °C and 5,000 m the 400 Hz harmonic shifts to 516 Hz.

  • Wind from the array itself: two-way chirps between the halves, t_ab = d/(c+u), t_ba = d/(c−u); 39 µs per m/s at d = 2 m. Needs the shared clock.
  • Limit found: picking the right grating lobe in one snapshot works only up to a 4–5 m baseline; 10–20 m needs multi-hypothesis tracking.
  • Cost: node ≈ ₹24,700 (estimate).

04 · Idea 2 (secondary) · Satellite forward-scatter corridor gate

Gate

Crossings at the choke points

Designed · not built

Principle. A drone crossing the satellite-to-receiver line scatters the signal strongly forward (Babinet’s principle). The sensor is receive-only and gives crossings, not tracks. Gate width is the Fresnel radius, √(λ·d).

Siting. Terrain that blinds radar also funnels a hidden drone into passes, nallahs and valley floors. Gates sit forward at those choke points, not on a perimeter: warning comes from where the gate is placed, not from how far it sees.

ConfigurationHardwareNotes
Field (operational)Ku-band geostationary beacon, single channel, fixed baseline≈ 49× the radar cross-section of GPS L1 at 11 GHz; the dish rejects ground jammers; the LNB needs 13–18 V but the RTL-SDR v4 bias-tee gives 4.5 V, so a separate inserter is required
Bench / developmentOne patch antenna, one RTL-SDR v4, GNSS-SDROne channel is enough because the reference is a software replica of the public C/A code

Signal and discrimination

  • Signature: a Doppler chirp of ≈ 17 Hz/s, from v²/(λ·d_r). A rate, not a frequency, so it is matched-filterable
  • A real crossing appears on one satellite; faults, scintillation or cloud appear on all. The receiver processes ≥ 4 satellites and rejects anything common
  • A jump in the GNSS noise floor on every satellite at once is reported as a hostile-EW alert, not a failure
  • Receiver thermal drift cancels in the cross-correlation

05 · Neutralisation strand

Deny

“Deny at the choke point, not through the hill.” All numbers are computed, not measured.

1B · Gate-triggered choke-point jammer

  • The lead mode, keyed only on a gate crossing
  • 10 mW nominal (100 mW ceiling) on L1; 0.1–1 W per C2 band (2.4 / 5.8 GHz) into a sector antenna down the pass
  • ≈ 14 µW breaks L1 lock at 80 m; denial bubble ≈ 2 km in-beam at 10 mW, contained by the valley walls
  • 1 W jammer vs a 100 mW controller: C2 J/S ≈ 41 dB. Losing C2 usually triggers return-to-home

2C · Ridge-crest slave effector

  • The fallback: D4’s own jammer or laser on the crest to restore line of sight
  • L1 then needs ≈ 8.7 mW instead of 71 W
  • The crest head is the most exposed node

3A · Jamming through the ridge · rejected

  • ≈ 71 W for GNSS alone, with ≈ 180 km of line-of-sight collateral denial
  • ≈ 6.3 kW (2.4 GHz) or ≈ 15 kW (5.8 GHz) for C2

Why not spoofing?

Ruled out by cue accuracy (±2°, ~140 m cross-range at 4 km) and by collateral, not by power.

08 · Judge stress test · nine attacks and our answers

Stress test

AttackAnswer
“You answered a different problem statement”Performance is the probability of detecting, tracking and engaging in time. We harden DRDO’s hardware and fix terrain blinding. Cueing cuts the EO search area by ≈ 50× (±3° → a 6°×6° box), and the masking flag stops wasted dwell.
“Detecting what you can’t engage only buys seconds”Yes, about 20 s. So the argument is track continuity through pop-up-and-peek and a pre-pointed effector, not lead time.
“Your own jammer kills the GNSS tripwire”The Ku beacon is the field configuration and its dish rejects ground jammers; there is a BLANK input and look-through windows; GPS jamming becomes an alert.
“A satellite tripwire contradicts your occlusion argument”It is a corridor gate. Terrain funnels hidden drones into passes, and the gates sit forward at those choke points.
“Knife-edge diffraction is the wrong model for sound”ITU-R P.526 is used only for the radio-versus-optics comparison. The node measures wind itself and reports wind shadows in a live confidence map; range is stated as wind-dependent.
“Your bearing points at the ridge, not the drone”DEM-aware localisation predicts the bending (roadmap). Arrivals at crest height are flagged as diffracted.
“Your threat model is assumed”Terrain-hugging is how drones beat radar. The harmonic comb detects any rotor or propeller.
“You’ve multiplied failure points”Nodes go only in the dead ground, so losing one loses one sector. Self-test runs every 10 minutes, and one person can set up a node.
“What’s actually new?”Terrain-aware C-UAS, plus the five contributions above.

Why not a radar on the ridge?

A radar emits, so it can be direction-found. A valley post has several dead sectors, which means several radars on exposed ridges. One acoustic sector costs ≈ ₹25k; a radar costs crores.

Fixed-wing drones or piston-engined munitions?

The comb detector finds any harmonic series.

11 · Context

Context

Built for Smart India Hackathon 2026, problem statement SIH26050: “High Altitude Performance Optimization and Robust Design of Anti-Drone System” (DRDO · Robotics and Drones · Hardware). I lead team GOODFELLASS (Team ID 152794) through the idea-submission round.

The system is the first and only product of Aeroscan Defence Systems Pvt. Ltd., my defence-tech startup, which is being proposed to the startup incubation cell at Sathyabama Institute of Science & Technology.

An earlier version fused RF, radar, EO/IR and acoustic sensors with weather-based weighting. It was dropped in favour of the 8-microphone acoustic array plus the corridor gate, which address occlusion directly.
