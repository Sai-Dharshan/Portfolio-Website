01 · The problem

Problem

Drones are hard to detect in high-altitude terrain. Counter-drone setups that assume a clear view of the sky run into two problems in the mountains.

01Line-of-sight blockage

Ridges and valleys hide a drone from sensors that need a direct view of it.

02Fog and snow

Low visibility blinds cameras, the sensors best at confirming what a target is.

02 · What I built

Approach

01Multi-sensor fusion

No single sensor works in every condition, so the system combines four: RF, radar, EO/IR and acoustic. Each covers a gap the others leave, and their detections are fused into one decision.

02Weather-based sensor weighting

Trust shifts between sensors as visibility changes: cameras count for more in clear conditions, and as fog or snow sets in the decision leans on the sensors that don’t need to see the target.

03Designed for mountain terrain

The design targets line-of-sight blockage and fog/snow, the two conditions that break conventional counter-drone setups at altitude.

03 · The sensor stack

Sensors

Each sensor’s strength covers another’s blind spot.

Radio frequency

RF

Picks up the radio link between a drone and its controller. It doesn’t need to see the drone, so fog and snow don’t stop it.

Active sensing

Radar

Tracks the range and motion of targets, and keeps working through fog and snow.

Electro-optical / infrared

EO/IR

Daylight and thermal cameras that confirm what a target is. Strongest in clear conditions, which is why its weight drops as visibility falls.

Microphone arrays

Acoustic

Listens for rotor noise and its direction, a cue that doesn’t depend on visibility.
